## The two magic-user bundles — the provenance defect is fixed

`INT-20260812-002` rejected `magic-user-spells-1-4-r05` and
`magic-user-spells-5-9-r05` because the GUP on disk no longer matched the
checksum its Review had pinned. **That is resolved.** Both were re-issued rather
than re-pinned — r07 and r06 respectively — and every checksum, component and
operation-index check now passes. Their indexes are exactly well formed: 233
entries over 233 rows, 210 over 210, each carrying `csv_row`.

They are blocked on three node identities that no approved packet has registered:

| node ID | bundle | CSV row |
|---|---|---|
| `spell_charm_person_or_mammal` | `magic-user-spells-1-4-r07` | 164 |
| `spell_heat_metal` | `magic-user-spells-1-4-r07` | 204 |
| `spell_pyrotechnics` | `magic-user-spells-5-9-r06` | 163 |

All three are registered by `GUP-PKT-PHB-054-064-druid-spells-r01`, which is
currently the one **blocked** item in the Builder queue: its Review escalations
are decided, but the deciding Decision orders another artifact first. So this is
an ordering dependency on the druid-spells packet, not a defect in either
magic-user bundle.

This is not ID drift. `spell_charm_person` exists in the registry and
`spell_charm_person_or_mammal` is the distinct druid spell; the druid packet
registers it. Whether those two identities are correctly distinct is a question
for constitution 3.2 and belongs to the Architect, not to integration — I raise
it only because 3.2 names singular/variant pairs as the most common defect in
this graph, and this pair sits exactly on that line.

**Remediation — Builder.** Unblock and publish `druid-spells`, or, if these three
spells are genuinely named by PHB 64–94, register them in the magic-user packets
and have the Reviews approve the additional registrations. Once the identities
exist, both bundles should apply unchanged.

Note that `magic-user-spells-5-9-r06` also depends on `magic-user-spells-1-4-r07`:
seven of its endpoints resolve only once r07 registers them. The two must be
integrated together or in that order.

## Applier note — agreeing redeclaration of an approved identity

All three ready bundles independently declared the node `rule_hit_dice`, and two
also declared `monster_extra_dimensional`. Every declaration agreed exactly on
ID, label and kind, and each was approved by its own Review. Packets are worked
in parallel, so two of them needing the same new identity is ordinary.

The applier refused the second declaration as an attempted overwrite of an
approved identity. That was too strict: the registry is the list of approved node
IDs (constitution 3.2), so one identity needs exactly one row, and writing it a
second time would have been the actual defect.

This holds on both paths, and both are now handled the same way:

- **within one batch** — the first declaration registers the identity, and a
  later identical one is satisfied; and
- **against the existing registry** — a packet may declare an identity that an
  earlier integration already registered. `rule_hit_dice` is exactly this case:
  `INT-20260815-001` registered it as `("Hit Dice", "rule")` an hour before these
  bundles were verified, and both magic-user bundles declare it identically.

A **disagreeing** redeclaration — the same ID with a different label or kind —
still aborts the batch on either path, because choosing between two definitions
of one identity is not an integration decision. Both branches are covered by
`tooling/integrator/tests/test_registration_agreement.py`, and both were
mutation-checked: disabling either guard fails a test.

Consequence for the two magic-user bundles: this was a *second*, independent
blocker sitting behind the missing druid spells. With it resolved, the missing
identities above are the only thing left between them and integration.
