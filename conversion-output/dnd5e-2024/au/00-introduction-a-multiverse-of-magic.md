## Alustriel's Arcana

In this book, the archmage Alustriel Silverhand from the Forgotten Realms setting—a human daughter of the god of magic Mystra and paragon of righteousness and virtue—presents arcana she has discovered during her travels. (You can learn more about Alustriel in *Vecna: Eve of Ruin.*)

But the contents of this book can be used in any setting, whether it's the Forgotten Realms, Ravenloft, or one of your own making. Think of this book as a toy box full of options to incorporate into your game as the Dungeon Master chooses.

This book relies on the rules in the *Player's Handbook,**Dungeon Master's Guide,* and *Monster Manual.*

### Schools of Magic

The eight schools of magic that underlie spellcraft are key to this book's magical character options, and its magical factions and tools for building magical worlds are tied to the schools' nuanced characteristics. The following entries expand on the schools' brief descriptions in the *Player's Handbook:*

1. **Abjuration** spells include protective and healing magic, as well as magic that prevents or reverses unwanted effects.
2. **Conjuration** spells involve creating objects and effects, summoning creatures, or teleporting.
3. **Divination** spells allow the caster to peer into the future or other planes of existence to gain helpful or bolstering benefits or information.
4. **Enchantment** spells include magic that bends targets' minds to the caster's will.
5. **Evocation** spells create physical effects that are often spectacular and might include destructive energy such as fire, cold, and acid.
6. **Illusion** spells include magic that creates sounds, images, smells, and other sensory effects that deceive the mind or senses.
7. **Necromancy** spells manipulate life and death, including restoring the dead to life, animating undead creatures, and siphoning life from the living.
8. **Transmutation** spells include magic that transforms creatures and objects, granting characteristics and abilities they previously lacked.

## Using This Book

Chapters 1 and 2 include character creation and play options and are suitable for all players; the remainder of this book is intended for DMs only. The chapters cover the following topics:

- **Chapter 1: Character Options.** This chapter presents eight subclasses, ten backgrounds, and more than two dozen feats perfect for creating characters who inhabit a magical world.
- **Chapter 2: Spells.** Dozens of spells offer new options for spellcasting heroes.
- **Chapter 3: Creating Magical Worlds.** This chapter presents guidelines for making magic a central part of your game, with examples from D&D settings, and examples of magical group patrons to inspire your games in any high-magic realm.
- Chapter 4: Magical Factions. Nine organizations of magic aficionados—one affiliated with each school of magic, plus one magical saboteur group—provide powerful allies or fearsome foes.
- **Chapter 5: Magic Items.** This chapter presents myriad new magic items to fill your magical hoards, plus rules for magic items that grow in power alongside the player characters.
- **Chapter 6: Magical Creatures.** This bestiary includes statistics for eight specialized archmages, minions warped by foul magic, living spells, the fiendish villain known as **Venger**, and more.
- **Appendix: Tomb of the Shadow Serpents.** This adventure for level 1 characters presents a challenge from **Venger** to claim treasure or die trying.

>
> **The Weave of Magic**
>
>
> The worlds within the D&D multiverse are magical places. All existence is suffused with magical power, and potential energy lies untapped in every rock, stream, and living creature—even in the air itself. Raw magic is present in every manifestation of energy throughout the multiverse.
>
> Mortals can't directly shape this raw magic. Instead, they use a fabric of magic—an interface between the will of a spellcaster and raw magic. The spellcasters of the Forgotten Realms call it the Weave and recognize its essence as the god Mystra, but casters have varied ways of naming and conceptualizing this interface.
>
> Whenever a magic effect is created, the threads of the Weave intertwine, twist, and fold to make the effect possible. A spellcaster surrounded by the Weave can shape lightning to blast foes, transport hundreds of miles in the blink of an eye, or even reverse death.
>
> But in places where the Weave is damaged, magic works in unpredictable ways—and if it's torn, raw magic is inaccessible. Even the most powerful archmage can't light a candle via magic in an area where the Weave has been torn.
