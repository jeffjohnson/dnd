# Chapter 4: Magical Factions


![Mages engage in a magical duel while members of the Crucible Keepers look on. (Art: Eilene Cherie)](book/AU/043-04-001.ch-4-splash.webp)

Many scholars and practitioners of magic seek ever-greater prowess and status, but few can achieve these individually. Instead, those pursuing mystical knowledge often join influential groups. Numerous in membership and great in achievement, these factions accept adventurers into their folds, offering resources and knowledge in exchange for loyalty and service.

Each faction described in this chapter has its own outlook and goals, such as providing magical advice and entertainment to influence the powerful, researching alien forces from beyond the stars, and uncovering the secrets of life and undeath.

Faction members exchange magical discoveries, mentor pupils, and learn from more experienced colleagues. Members are united by shared specialties and philosophies. Although these organizations' disparate objectives often bring them into conflict with each other, members of different factions might also unite over common goals.

You can use these factions as patrons, as villains, or as organizations the characters can join. Adapt them to your world or campaign as needed.

*[Page 79]*

## Faction Descriptions

This chapter includes details on the following factions:

- **Bejeweled Conclave.** The discreet enchanters of the Bejeweled Conclave make themselves invaluable to powerful clients as elite entertainers, advisers, and mediators while amassing clients' secrets.
- **Bringers of Cosmic Dawn.** Reclusive transmuters obsessed with mutations, the Bringers of Cosmic Dawn seek guidance from alien intelligences.
- **Covenant of the Grave.** The necromancers of the Covenant of the Grave study death and undeath with scientific precision, seeking esoteric knowledge through rigorous research.
- **Crucible Keepers.** From volcanic eruptions to shore-shredding hurricanes, no natural disaster is too dangerous for the daring Crucible Keepers to study in their pursuit of ever-escalating applications for Evocation magic.
- **Horizon Weavers.** The Horizon Weavers' conjurers practice summoning and teleportation as they explore and chart the multiverse.
- **Ninth Quill.** The spies and saboteurs of the Ninth Quill steal lore and artifacts from other powerful mages; many members do so to safeguard these treasures, but others are motivated by greed. The Ninth Quill also stokes dissent among the other factions to keep them from growing too powerful.
- **Phantasmic Circus.** The showy illusionists of the Phantasmic Circus entertain eager audiences with impossible spectacles.
- **Seers of Sea and Sky.** The diligent oracles of the Seers of Sea and Sky divine truths in the movements of the wind and waves.
- **Sheltering Hands.** The abjurers of the Sheltering Hands uplift the downtrodden and protect those in need.

## Bejeweled Conclave

The Bejeweled Conclave is known as an organization of professional entertainers and mediators who cater to wealthy clients. They host glamorous parties, serve as courtiers, broker deals, and provide entertainment for exclusive functions.

But the Bejeweled Conclave wields far more influence than its publicly known services indicate. Consummate agents behind the throne, the enchanters of the Bejeweled Conclave cultivate elite connections and shift the balance of power in cities across the world.

*[Page 81]*

### Goals and Activities

![This unassuming baker is, in reality, a member of the Bejeweled Conclave's Whisper Network. (Art: Gaboleps)](book/AU/045-04-003.bejeweled-conclave-baker.webp)

The Bejeweled Conclave seeks to increase its sway across the multiverse. The faction engages skilled diplomats, courtiers, and entertainers and hones their abilities. Enchanters and spies receive a more clandestine, but no less thorough, education. The conclave prizes subtlety and secrecy above all; members are taught that it's better to fail a mission and keep the conclave's secrets than to succeed at the cost of exposing the faction's true goals.

The Bejeweled Conclave operates in major cities throughout the multiverse. Distance keeps members from knowing all their peers, but the faction is quick to celebrate successes, building loyalty and camaraderie within its ranks. While all members learn conversational tricks to offer convincing praise, the compliments members bestow on each other are usually genuine.

Conclave members understand that overtly using Enchantment magic risks exposing them as more than entertainers and courtiers. If a member must modify a noble's memories or magically dominate a general, they undertake significant planning to conceal their involvement.

*[Page 82]*

### Leadership and Organization

More experienced members of the Bejeweled Conclave oversee operations assigned to junior members. Valuable secrets are reported through this hierarchy up to the faction's highest ranks, referred to as the Whisper Network. These leaders hoard a treasure trove of secrets; there are few facts about the movers and shakers of civilization that the Bejeweled Conclave doesn't know.

Conclave members are trusted to act alone when necessary, such as when an important client insists on hiring only a single mediator or companion. When possible, though, senior members politely request that their "junior clerks" or "associates in training" handle more workaday aspects of their affairs. Such requests, which might include subtle magical compulsion, are a ruse to bring another trained member to operate from the shadows. As a result, clients who planned to planned to hire only one member of the Bejeweled Conclave often find themselves engaging two or three.

*[Page 82]*

#### Whisper Network

Members of the Whisper Network keep their identities private, even from their peers. The only exception is Madame Hestia Ilverne (Medium **Noble Prodigy**), a courteous human Whisper Network member who heads the conclave's public-facing efforts. Rumors within the faction claim that Madame Ilverne is among the lowest ranked of the leaders, but she seems nothing but pleased with her position.

Whether members of the Whisper Network work as music instructors, clerks, dancers, or cooks, they keep an eye on all aspects of Bejeweled Conclave activities. If they have edicts or missions to impart, they do so through Madame Ilverne. When she casually mentions a task to a member with the words "The Network would prefer a favor," all comprehend the seriousness of the request.

*[Page 82]*

#### Lurewood Mansion

A regal manor house in the garden district of a major city, Lurewood Mansion is the conclave's academy. The previous owners donated their property generations ago for unknown reasons. The manor's architecture has been adapted to include numerous salons and studies, as well as a fine kitchen.

In the studies, members of the Bejeweled Conclave learn to negotiate deals and defuse arguments. In the salons, singers, dancers, and musicians practice entertainment suitable for elevated private parties, from subdued chamber music to sensual dances to heart-wrenching ballads. New faction members take lessons here no matter how polished they are when recruited, if for no other reason than to bolster the illusion that negotiation and entertainment are the Bejeweled Conclave's prime objectives.

Beneath the mansion, a dizzying maze of chambers connected through locked doors and one-way mirrors facilitates practice in eavesdropping, lock-picking, quick changes, and stealthy escapes. Other rooms allow members to rest out of sight. The heart of the maze contains the mansion's best-kept secret: workrooms where enchanters teach apprentice mages the arts of twisting minds, magically hiding secrets, and erasing memories.

*[Page 83]*

#### Joining the Conclave

Aspiring faction members can't join the Bejeweled Conclave by requesting admission; members inevitably deny that there are any openings in what they claim is simply a loose professional organization.

Entry is only by invitation. Senior members carefully watch entertainers and mediators who have impressive public reputations. Those who seem particularly shrewd and ambitious might receive an invitation for free training in their specialties at Lurewood Mansion, hosted by Madame Ilverne.

Once an invitee arrives, Madame Ilverne engages them in cryptic conversation. If the potential member impresses her with their poise, acumen, and discretion, she invites them to join the faction. Otherwise, she thanks them for their time and sends them on their way without ever indicating that the mansion is anything other than a professional academy.

*[Page 83]*

## Bringers of Cosmic Dawn

![A member of the Bringers of Cosmic Dawn transforms a colleague into a strange creature. (Art: Pedro Correa)](book/AU/046-04-004.bringers-of-cosmic-dawn.webp)

A secretive organization of amoral transmuters, the Bringers of Cosmic Dawn receives abstruse directives from alien entities that hail from the dark spaces between stars. Faction members magically mutate plants and creatures in preparation for visitation by these beings—an event they believe is inevitable. They believe these entities will witness their glorious transmutations, be impressed, and bring about an exalted future full of mutated aberrations that serve transmuters' every whim.

*[Page 84]*

### Goals and Activities

The exact goals of the Bringers of Cosmic Dawn are inscrutable to many members, though all believe they are helping to usher an ascended age of existence. The faction draws unprincipled transmuters, fringe scientists, and irrational astronomers to its academy and observatory, the Cosmic Oculus, where they receive fractured instructions from the faction's five leaders, known as the Starspeakers.

These communications have a consistent message: Faction members must transmute the flora and fauna surrounding the Cosmic Oculus. Invariably, this involves transforming plants into hostile, flesh-eating specimens and mutating beasts to rearrange organs, add joints to limbs, and cause them to secrete slime. In doing so, the faction hopes to gain the attention of alien entities they believe will descend and warp all worlds in their path.

The faction members are united in purpose but not in execution. Some pursue their own theories of mutation and transfiguration, experimenting on themselves as much as on other subjects. Some instructions communicated by the Starspeakers conflict with others, as though the alien entities they seek to communicate with weren't working in concert. These contradictions mean that the faction's work advances erratically, with rejected experiments as common as transformations deemed to be in concordance with the aliens' will.

*[Page 85]*

#### Dawnrise Canyon

Bringers of Cosmic Dawn members carry out most transmutations far from public scrutiny. The faction is based in the secluded Dawnrise Canyon, far below the clifftop where the Cosmic Oculus stands. The once-tranquil canyon is now a dangerous valley of frightening creatures, hostile plants, aggressive transmuters, and wandering aberrations drawn to the unnatural landscape. Time moves erratically in the region thanks to frequent use of time-altering magic to speed up or slow down specific mutations.

*[Page 85]*

#### Opposition to the Far Realm

While outsiders might not comprehend the distinction between the alien intelligences the Starspeakers commune with and the monsters of the Far Realm, Bringers of Cosmic Dawn members fear and reject the Far Realm as unknowable, indescribable, and inimical to life. The faction works to counter Far Realm incursions and expels any members who deal directly with that domain of incomprehensible horror.

*[Page 86]*

### Leadership and Organization

The Bringers of the Cosmic Dawn lacks a rigid hierarchy, but its leaders maintain careful control over the information they receive from both members' experiments and alien intelligences.

*[Page 86]*

#### Member Roles

Certain members of the Bringers of Cosmic Dawn have special roles in the faction.

*[Page 86]*

##### Starspeakers

The five enigmatic Starspeakers command the entire faction. None answer to any name besides their title, and they rarely leave the Cosmic Oculus except to measure progress within Dawnrise Canyon. Four Starspeakers (Medium **Aberrant Cultist**) are human. The fifth and most senior Starspeaker appears to be an elderly human astronomer, but she is actually a magically disguised **Aboleth** who has eschewed the Far Realm.

*[Page 86]*

##### Worldwarpers

Most faction members work at the Cosmic Oculus or Dawnrise Canyon, but others, called Worldwarpers, venture into civilized lands to engage in more far-flung experimentation. Worldwarpers seek out willing recipients to, as they put it, "gain blessings from beyond the stars."

Worldwarpers might offer to improve a banker's garden with voracious plants to deter thieves, provide an aberration to help guard a prison, or augment a diplomat's body to produce intoxicating pheromones. Like the work in Dawnrise Canyon, these puzzling changes ostensibly further a wider agenda, but Worldwarpers are rarely aware of the end goals; they merely put pieces in place as commanded and report back to the Starspeakers.

*[Page 86]*

##### Blots

The Starspeakers secretly assign faction members to find and eradicate Far Realm sympathizers. These individuals, known within the faction as Blots, also venture into places where the Far Realm's stain bleeds into the world, seeking to quash the Far Realm's presence wherever they find it.

#### Joining the Bringers

Transmuters might seek out the Bringers of Cosmic Dawn even if they don't wish to advance the faction's goals. Faction members are undeniable masters of Transmutation magic, and some daring characters might join long enough to learn their secrets. Although some transmuters escape the faction, far too many begin to believe the Starspeakers' ramblings and become devotees—or worse, they become unwilling subjects for experimental mutations when their disloyalty is uncovered.

*[Page 86]*

#### Cosmic Oculus

Once an abandoned observatory, the Cosmic Oculus became the Bringers of Cosmic Dawn's base when the faction discovered that the facility seems to magically amplify the messages of entities between the stars. In addition to the Starspeakers, faction astronomers also live in the Cosmic Oculus to record psychic signals from deep space and make nightly measurements of celestial bodies.

Most faction members never see the inside of the Cosmic Oculus. The ramshackle buildings clustered along the cliff face between the observatory and the canyon have expanded and mutated on their own, and members have used spells such as **Stone Shape** and **Fabricate** to haphazardly extend walls, enclose laboratories, and create passageways through the rock.

## Covenant of the Grave

The Covenant of the Grave is a loose affiliation of necromancers and those interested in studying death and undeath through a disciplined, scientific lens. The covenant has no room for megalomaniacs who seek domination via hordes of undead minions.

These realists are aware that their studies, no matter how clinical, unnerve most people, so their members are unobtrusive and their laboratories hidden. The greatest covenant academy is the Pale Tower, a morbid, bone-white structure far from civilization.

*[Page 88]*

### Goals and Activities

Covenant members are typically more interested in deepening their knowledge than their power. They freely share their research with each other via correspondence, personal visits, or clandestine lectures, but they rarely reveal discoveries to those outside the organization. Many worry that their research might be misused for sinister, nonacademic ends. Others simply don't expect anyone but like-minded scholars to comprehend their work.

Unlike necromancers who incite undead uprisings, the Covenant of the Grave usually works to stymie undead hordes and return less powerful undead to the safer confines of the laboratory or grave. Members consider undead rampages dreadfully unscientific, if not outright embarrassing. They're keenly aware of the harm that such villainous necromancy brings to the faction's reputation. Their opposition to necromantic wickedness sometimes puts the faction in conflict with powerful undead such as liches, death knights, or vampires.

*[Page 88]*

### Leadership and Organization

Although an established hierarchy exists within the Covenant of the Grave, members are usually too engrossed in their own research to worry about rank.

*[Page 88]*

#### Necrodemics and Gravetenders

Any faction member who shares a significant scholarly truth about death, decay, or undeath with the faction might gain the title of Necrodemic. Some members hold expanded titles such as Arisen Necrodemic or Necrodemic Embalmist, awarded for sharing more complex knowledge.

The title of Gravetender is held by the three head administrators of the Pale Tower. Emeritus administrators keep this title after retirement, so four to six Gravetenders usually exist at a time. Gravetenders set the overall direction for the faction but are usually bookish and circumspect, so change within the faction is gradual.

*[Page 89]*

#### Joining the Covenant

![Members of the Covenant of the Grave show respect to undead individuals who allow themselves to be studied. (Art: Johan Grenier)](book/AU/047-04-005.covenant-of-the-grave.webp)

Any spellcaster with an interest in Necromancy magic or macabre research is likely to hear about the Covenant of the Grave, but the faction doesn't actively recruit. The covenant expects aspirants to independently identify its members and present a well-reasoned proposal for membership. A member who receives a qualified aspirant usually confers with other members before either taking on the aspirant as a research assistant or sending them to study at the Pale Tower.

*[Page 89]*

Only a few members of the Covenant of the Grave are undead. Covenant members have little use for less powerful undead such as skeletons and zombies, and other undead are usually too consumed by their hungers to maintain the intellectual rigor the covenant expects. ## Crucible Keepers

The Crucible Keepers consists of daredevil evokers who venture into natural or magical disasters to study the phenomenal power at play. Some members use their Evocation magic to counter disasters and help others, while other members revel in nature's destructive forces and take inspiration from them to enhance their own devastating spellcraft.

*[Page 92]*

### Goals and Activities

![Crucible Keepers love a good explosion. (Art: Swatches Studio)](book/AU/049-04-007.crucible-keepers.webp)

Crucible Keepers study Evocation magic to command energy and control powerful forces. They eschew solitary scholarship, preferring informal gatherings where they exchange ideas and try to impress their peers with spectacular displays of magical might.

Faction members might be thrill seekers, but they aren't fools. They plan their dangerous endeavors well in advance, spending days or weeks charting natural or magical phenomena before enjoying the rush of soaring through the eye of a hurricane or surfing a lava flow. They employ protective magic while engaging in these endeavors, and they're quick to chide fools who confront danger unprepared.

> *[Page 92]*
>
> > Overuse of powerful evocation magic can warp the land as well as the souls of those who become drunk on magical power. The Crucible Keepers' attempt to right these wrongs is noble.
> > — *Alustriel*

*[Page 93]*

### Leadership and Organization

Those drawn to the Crucible Keepers aren't the sort who like following orders. Ranks within the Crucible Keepers are unofficial, fluid, and based on frequent competitive challenges between members, with losers acknowledging winners' higher rank. Challenges usually involve magical duels, elaborate races, or dangerous stunts.

*[Page 93]*

#### Joining the Crucible Keepers

The Crucible Keepers enthusiastically welcomes anyone who wants to try out for its ranks. Gaining membership is as simple as impressing any faction member with a clever display of Evocation magic, a wild physical stunt, or a dramatic parlor trick.

*[Page 93]*

#### Eljar Charscale

The current head of the Crucible Keepers is a dragonborn named Eljar Charscale (Medium **Evoker Archmage**; see **chapter 6**). Originally a human, Eljar was executed by cruel generals when he refused to use his magic in an unpopular war. After his allies reincarnated him, Eljar fled in his new dragonborn body. He took the name Charscale on joining the Crucible Keepers.

Eljar loves the thrill of Evocation magic and is undefeated in duels of spellcraft. In addition to his magical talent, Eljar is respected for listening to everyone and offering sensible guidance. He began leading the Crucible Keepers only a few months ago after the former leader died in a forest fire.

Eljar has a strong antiwar stance that he disseminates throughout the organization. He has forbidden members of the Crucible Keepers from serving as military battle mages. Some evokers believe this stance restricts their career paths, but most understand the reasoning behind Eljar's policy.

Although members believe that nature is the best classroom, the Crucible Keepers maintains a learning institution focused on Evocation magic. The Caldera Arcadium is a large stone building hanging over the rim of an active volcano, suspended by enormous metal chains and struts.

The temperature inside the building ranges from unpleasantly warm to blisteringly hot, but eager students fill its three classrooms every day. The building also holds magical forges for the study and creation of magic items. A stone bridge from the Arcadium's roof leads to dormitories, academic offices, and a cafeteria, all hanging off the volcano's edge.

The Caldera Arcadium's headmaster is a scholarly **Efreeti** named Nadia Flameheart, who taught magic in the City of Brass for a millennium before joining the Crucible Keepers. The only wishes she entertains are those for better grades, and her only responses are tips for studying.

The Scorchborn are also instrumental to the wards that protect the school from volcanic eruptions, but not even Nadia Flameheart understands how. Arcadium students frequently engage in death-defying stunts they call "field trips." ## Horizon Weavers

The Horizon Weavers is a collection of far-flung conjurers, explorers, and scholars who practice summoning, teleportation, and planar travel.

Members of the Horizon Weavers have unparalleled wanderlust; they've seen sights and lived through experiences most would consider too wild to be true. However, the faction's planar sojourns have not gone unnoticed, and members now work to avert the attention of a malevolent extraplanar entity fixated on their organization.

*[Page 96]*

### Goals and Activities

Horizon Weavers hone their expertise in all forms of Conjuration magic, from summoning to creation to teleportation, with a focus on extraplanar travel. Powerful Horizon Weavers create gates between planes, while junior members catalog known gates or simply wander through to see what's on the other side. New faction members usually begin by traveling their own world to experience different climates and cultures before exploring other planes.

Many faction members are also amateur or professional artisans; some weave textiles as well as magic. Horizon Weavers often incorporate spells such as **Find Familiar** or **Mage Hand** into their everyday lives, using magic for everything from companionship to household chores.

*[Page 96]*

#### Horizon Weaver Cloaks

Each new member who joins the faction receives a durable cloak with their initials sewn into the collar. Horizon Weavers recognize these garments on sight and seek to retrieve any that are lost or stolen.

A Horizon Weaver's cloak repels water and protects its wearer from most types of weather. The faction replaces damaged cloaks when necessary. Some members weave the threads of former cloaks into new ones.

Many Horizon Weavers incorporate their cloaks into magic items they create. The most popular choices are **Cape of the Mountebank**, **Cloak of Protection**, and **Clothes of Mending**.

*[Page 96]*

#### The Lurker

A massive beast from the liminal space between planes menaces the Horizon Weavers. This entity, known as the Lurker, towers higher than mountains, with immense pincers and a single, enormous eye that radiates malice and reflects strange lights and lines.

Several months ago, when faction members first glimpsed the Lurker, a powerful team of Horizon Weavers set out to confront the monster. The team went missing, and the Lurker has since become more aggressive, snapping at some Horizon Weavers in the split second it takes them to magically travel between planes. Sometimes this shears the unlucky traveler in half; other times, the individual never reappears. These incidents are rare, but all Horizon Weavers worry about a potential Lurker strike with each planar trip.

> *[Page 96]*
>
> > Those who provide shelter and aid to explorers of this vast multiverse are friends to both me and Mystra.
> > — *Alustriel*

### Leadership and Organization

As befits members of a society of explorers, Horizon Weavers are widely scattered. A wandering Horizon Weaver might go weeks or months without seeing another member, and a meeting might be coincidental. Senior faction members, called Gatemakers, settle disputes or provide advice, although reaching a Gatemaker often requires a cross-planar trek.

## Ninth Quill

![Members of the Ninth Quill plot the heist of magical lore. (Art: Xavier Leroux)](book/AU/053-04-011.ninth-quill-plotting.webp)

The Ninth Quill is a faction of thieves, saboteurs, and spies who use magic to obtain and sequester powerful artifacts and knowledge by any means necessary—including robbing and sabotaging other factions. The faction's leaders consider their organization to be superior to factions focused on a specific school of magic and the safest custodian of dangerous lore and relics, but some members also steal valuables and undermine other organizations for personal gain.

*[Page 100]*

### Goals and Activities

Ninth Quill members' adaptability and diverse areas of expertise help them infiltrate other groups, often posing as new recruits or long-absent members from other lands.

The Ninth Quill has a few popular catchphrases that members use as meaningful mantras—or excuses for criminality. These phrases are described in the sections that follow.

*[Page 100]*

#### Our Hands Are Safest

Ninth Quill members often steal magic items and research to keep them out of others' hands. They receive extensive training in handling dangerous artifacts and resisting the influence of sentient objects. In principle, these items are sequestered in the Ninth Quill vaults (which members insist are more secure than any other faction's), but less scrupulous members might keep certain items for their own use.

*[Page 100]*

#### Others Learn So We Can Earn

When the Ninth Quill steals discoveries and relics that aren't particularly harmful, it sells them to wealthy patrons to fund its operations. Desperate buyers rebuffed by other factions, such as a necromancer rejected by the **Covenant of the Grave** or a transmuter suspicious of the **Bringers of Cosmic Dawn**, might seek the services of the Ninth Quill to obtain such objects.

*[Page 100]*

#### Discontent Is Our Best Defense

The Ninth Quill seeks to destabilize other factions that become too powerful. This often involves agents infiltrating a faction, then undertaking whisper campaigns, sabotaging experiments, and encouraging critical personnel to depart. The Ninth Quill agents quietly slip away once they have stoked sufficient inefficiency, paranoia, and infighting.

*[Page 101]*

### Leadership and Organization

The Ninth Quill values intelligence, resourcefulness, and loyalty. A significant majority of Ninth Quill agents use spellcasting to enhance their spy craft.

*[Page 101]*

#### A Careful Hierarchy

Although Ninth Quill leaders take pains to hide their identities, the faction operates under a strict hierarchal system. Agents are the lowest ranking, followed by Lieutenants, followed by Masters. Those in the highest echelon are Guild Masters.

*[Page 101]*

#### Joining the Ninth Quill

The Ninth Quill operates in the shadows and therefore doesn't entertain applicants. However, the Guild Masters keep an eye on talented thieves and other morally ambiguous individuals with magical skills. If the Guild Masters believe such an individual would be an asset to the Ninth Quill, they extend an invitation to visit Blackscript College—through an anonymous courier, of course.

*[Page 101]*

#### Blackscript College

The hidden academy where the Ninth Quill trains its members is located in a crime-ridden neighborhood of a busy trade city. Dilapidated warehouses, all owned by the Ninth Quill, surround the college to ensure its privacy. There's no readily apparent entrance; instead, it's accessible only by unmarked doors in twisting alleys and hidden passages.

The centuries-old college was once a legitimate magical academy, but city records show that it closed decades ago due to administrative corruption. In truth, the college simply went underground to teach magical spy craft and thievery.

A former instructor at Blackscript College's predecessor academy, the college's dean is the elf Sirethe Slipshadow (Medium **Spy Master**). Slipshadow is infamous for her ability to impersonate others and uses these skills to observe students during their studies and on early missions.

The college's library, called the Repository, holds an eclectic variety of stolen treasures. An adjacent chamber called the Ersatz Repository contains worktables where members make high-quality counterfeits of spellbooks, relics, and artifacts. Many cherished treasures held by other factions are actually Ninth Quill counterfeits.

## Phantasmic Circus

![Members of the Phantasmic Circus prepare for a magic-enhanced performance. (Art: kelogsloops)](book/AU/054-04-012.phantasmic-circus-preparing.webp)

Part entertainers' alliance and part traveling carnival, the Phantasmic Circus is an organization of performers and illusionists who create grand spectacles and share magical techniques to delight, entertain, and misdirect.

*[Page 103]*

### Goals and Activities

The Phantasmic Circus stages elaborate performances across the world, moving from city to city to bring delight and wonder. These performances are huge extravaganzas involving dozens of entertainers and drawing large audiences. The circus is renowned for illusions and pyrotechnics that reach hundreds of feet into the air.

These complex routines change with every show; Phantasmic Circus performers are always experimenting with new illusions, death-defying stunts, and musical accompaniment. Tickets and concessions are pricey, but audience members remember the faction's thrilling spectacles for the rest of their lives.

The Phantasmic Circus's performances use trained creatures for certain acts, but audience members would be shocked to realize how few genuine creatures are on display. Lifelike illusions of animals and monsters perform most stunts, while living animals are treated carefully and kept out of danger.

*[Page 103]*

#### One Circus, Many Troupes

The Phantasmic Circus isn't a single traveling troupe, but rather the collective name for several groups that operate independently, each under the guidance of a manager called a Wondermaker. Circus members might shift from group to group to hone their skills, so residents of a city might never realize that the "Phantasmic Circus" that visited twice the previous year was actually two different troupes.

*[Page 104]*

### Leadership and Organization

The astounding and popular Phantasmic Circus of today had humble beginnings as a small traveling carnival called the Hayver and Hayver Circus. When the Hayvers' youngest sibling, the human Della, returned to the family circus with training in Illusion magic and a gregarious gnome husband named Walnip Waddlebell, the circus started its meteoric rise.

Soon, Della and Walnip were running the circus as its ringmasters and self-appointed "First Maestros." The couple also gave the increasingly illusion-focused show a flashier name. The other Hayvers have since passed away, but the aging First Maestros (both Small **Performer Legend**), still plan spectacles, train new illusionists, and orchestrate the many troupes operating as the Phantasmic Circus.

*[Page 104]*

#### Wondermakers

The First Maestros are now too old to tour, but they keep in touch with each troupe as its travels bring it back to the circus's headquarters, the Mirage Lyceum. The First Maestros appoint a Wondermaker to guide each troupe. Most Wondermakers are flashy showoffs or grand illusionists who enjoy the spotlight as ringmasters or star performers. In other troupes, the Wondermaker works behind the scenes, guiding the performers and pushing their shows to new heights.

Each Wondermaker keeps their troupe's books, protects its members, makes its travel plans, and coordinates with other Wondermakers to ensure they aren't revisiting cities too soon. They also send performers from troupe to troupe to teach gifted members additional skills or to resolve personality clashes.

*[Page 104]*

#### Roustabouts

Although acrobats, illusionists, animals, and entertainers are the stars of the Phantasmic Circus, a platoon of supporters keeps the troupe running. Collectively called roustabouts, these laborers and guides let the performers take the glory while collecting reasonable pay from the troupe's Wondermaker. The visual artists who design the elaborate sets and costumes are also roustabouts; they mentor each other to hone their artistic skills.

## Seers of Sea and Sky

The Seers of Sea and Sky is a faction of oracles who make predictions about the future based on movements of the clouds and waves. Open to all methods of divination, the seers encourage communication with a wide array of aerial and undersea prophets and meticulously record their prognostications.

*[Page 106]*

### Goals and Activities

The Seers of Sea and Sky maintains libraries and reading rooms in many large, coastal cities. Within these hubs, seers cast Divination spells for reasonable costs; train other members in divination techniques; and maintain contact with visionaries across, above, and below the seas. Although obtaining accurate information through divination is important to the faction, sharing knowledge and imparting guidance to those most in need of it is also a crucial part of the faction's mission.

The seers primarily practice what they call hydromancy, or predictions based on watching the movement of water, and aeromancy, or predictions based on observing the weather. Members hone many subspecialties within these techniques. For example, some seers divine by scrutinizing driftwood delivered by ocean waves, while others base predictions on the movement of clouds.

*[Page 106]*

#### Allies of Air and Water

Faction members live in diverse locales and include a wide array of folk. The faction's membership includes aarakocra, giant eagles, and other flying creatures who can provide detailed data about the wind and cloud movement. Other seers live beneath the waves, including bronze dragons and merfolk. Members from both groups and land-bound seers often convene, keeping the faction cohesive.

*[Page 106]*

#### Truth Twice

The seers maintain a philosophy they call "truth twice." The faction doesn't consider a prophecy or revelation to be reliable unless it's been corroborated by another member—preferably one using a different Divination spell or method of divining. Checking and rechecking other members' work is routine. As a result, the seers' predictions have a reputation for reliability. The support members provide for each other contributes to the organization's close-knit culture.

![Members of the Seers of Sea and Sky perform powerful Divination magic outdoors to reveal terrible threats. (Art: Alba BG)](book/AU/055-04-013.seers-divining.webp)

*[Page 107]*

### Leadership and Organization

The oracles who founded the Seers of Sea and Sky were called the Scholars of Deep Truth. As the scholars' fame spread and wealthy patrons sought their guidance, the scholars built an exclusive, opulent lighthouse to entertain petitioners. The scholars worked with undersea oracles to strengthen the accuracy of their own prophecies.

When several misleading prognostications left locals victims of suspicious seaside disasters, the scholars started questioning their sources. They soon discovered an **Aboleth** named Naurishaul was manipulating them. Before the scholars could address this shocking news, a client exposed the scholars as minions of this malevolent undersea entity. While technically true, this announcement was misleading, as the scholars hadn't realized their answers came from such a sinister source.

*[Page 107]*

#### A New Beginning

The scandal could have driven the scholars into hiding or ruin, but the organization publicly acknowledged its unsavory association and resolved to do better. Members established a network of mutual education and support, teaching genuine divination methods and rigorously double-checking their revelations. They kept the remote lighthouse as the faction's headquarters but established outposts to provide answers in seaside cities where petitioners of more limited means could seek guidance.

The efforts to rebuild the scholars' reputation worked. Experts in hydromancy and aeromancy joined the faction, attracted by its openness and support. Many were diviners from far-flung places, including windswept peaks and undersea communities. To reflect the organization's growing membership and shed its tainted legacy, the faction changed its name to the Seers of Sea and Sky.

Focused entirely on their divination mission, the seers eschew hierarchy. New members are expected to respect more senior members, but the faction in general has an egalitarian outlook. Beyond the Founders (see below), members consider titles and ranks unnecessary.

*[Page 107]*

#### The Founders

The faction's inner circle consists of the original Scholars of Deep Truth members, though they use that scandal-ridden name only as a cautionary tale. Other seers simply refer to them as the Founders. With a reputation for humility, insight, and rigor, the Founders are highly regarded throughout the faction.

The Founders consist of the human siblings Kessalee Cloudseeker and Narvok Cloudseeker (Medium **Mage**), haughty elf Travieste Nilvinarian (Medium **Noble Prodigy**), and gregarious gnome Bulrug Seawhistle (Small **Performer Maestro**). The Founders frequently travel to consult with faction members scattered across land, sea, and air environments.

*[Page 108]*

#### Wavewatchers

Some Seers of Sea and Sky have formed a coterie called the Wavewatchers. These members enjoy watching ocean waves crash against the shore, and they spend hours in contemplation, watching light play on the waves and listening to their soothing sounds. The Wavewatchers have created a meditation trend that attracts more faction members each week. A few of the newest faction members aren't diviners at all—they're mystics or monks who've joined to seek peace from the rolling waves.

However, some faction members have fallen behind in their work in favor of wave watching, and they're producing unconfirmed divinations. This is unacceptable to the Founders, who are displeased with the Wavewatchers phenomenon. Kessalee worries that their old foe Naurishaul has infiltrated the faction once again by mesmerizing susceptible faction members.

#### Joining the Seers of Sea and Sky

Anyone with an interest in studying the sea or sky to gain insights can join the faction as a scribe or assistant for the diviners who perform the bulk of the faction's work. Joining as a diviner is more difficult. Applicants must travel to the faction's lighthouse and request membership from any senior member, who makes the final admission decision. Those who don't qualify for membership are usually still welcome to stay at the lighthouse as guests.

*[Page 108]*

#### Lighthouse Academy

The prior incarnation of the Seers of Sea and Sky, the Scholars of Deep Truth, renovated a sprawling lighthouse as an upscale retreat for wealthy petitioners. The lighthouse remains the seers' headquarters and the Founders' home, but it has been renovated to align to the seers' new mission.

The Lighthouse Academy, as it's now known, houses dozens. New members have built outbuildings around the lighthouse and equipped them with bunk rooms, scrying chambers, and libraries.

Few residents of the Lighthouse Academy work alone; daily mentoring and mutual confirmation of predictions keep the seers in close contact with each other. Shared living spaces here outnumber individual residences.

The seers employ lighthouse keepers who know little about divination but are experts in cleaning and operating the facility. To further repair their reputation, the Founders recently had these operators construct two smaller lighthouses on nearby promontories. The new lighthouses provide additional navigational guidance to sea travelers. Many captains in turn give seers free passage, citing the value of the lighthouses while secretly believing that no seer would seek passage on a ship that wasn't destined to reach its destination safely.

### Using This Faction

The Seers of Sea and Sky are an excellent source of prophecies to steer a campaign. Faction members receive guidance from the natural world and freely share their divinations rather than hoard them. ## Sheltering Hands

The Sheltering Hands is a benevolent organization of abjurers and advocates who operate emergency shelters and soup kitchens in far-flung settlements. This well-respected faction uses Abjuration magic to protect the vulnerable and remove afflictions.

*[Page 110]*

### Goals and Activities

Sheltering Hands members are skilled abjurers who focus on providing services to the needy. Faction members solicit donations of food, money, or land, then share them in directed ways to reduce as much suffering as possible. They operate shelters, aid disease-stricken regions, and work to lift curses.

Beyond fulfilling immediate needs, members of the Sheltering Hands help individuals rebuild their dignity, security, and self-determination. Its members happily share bread with the hungry, but they also delight in helping the hungry set up communal farms and other enterprises so they can feed themselves. Members also work against despots to build community solidarity and create support programs that help marginalized populations voice their needs.

Abjurers of the Sheltering Hands also teach the rudiments of defensive magic to those who show interest and aptitude. Faction members who are mages lift curses and remove debilitating conditions; even members who can't cast the appropriate spells usually know enough to recognize what spells are needed.

Many of those the faction helps become members themselves, seeking to pay forward the support they've received and prevent others from suffering as they did.

*[Page 110]*

#### Balance of Faith

The Sheltering Hands isn't a religious organization, though some of its members are priests and many venerate deities of mercy, charity, and self-sacrifice. The faction doesn't favor any particular religion and welcomes good-hearted members of all sorts, including former followers of evil faiths who have renounced their earlier affiliations. This nonsectarian policy allows the Sheltering Hands to coordinate relief or rescue efforts across organizations.

*[Page 110]*

#### Fieldwork

Instead of expecting those in need to journey to them, small teams of Sheltering Hands members travel to deliver food, refresh supplies, and cast Abjuration spells. Spells from other schools of magic are often useful to repair structures, produce a bounty from sickly crops, or chase away opportunistic predators, so teams often include spellcasters with specialties other than Abjuration.

*[Page 110]*

#### Dueling Priorities

Friction within the Sheltering Hands comes from disagreements about how the organization might best help those in need. Some members believe the Sheltering Hands should focus on providing emergency aid to those experiencing hardship. Others believe that while providing aid is important, the organization should focus more on providing resources to communities to address societal struggles that cause long-term suffering. Some tension exists between these two groups, each of whose members believe their priority is superior. This dispute was minor at first, but some Sheltering Hands members have started refusing to serve alongside those of the opposite group, throwing off the faction's carefully planned administration.

#### Joining the Sheltering Hands

The Sheltering Hands welcomes all recruits interested in aiding the faction's mission, whether by offering protection during a faction outing or providing spellcasting services.

#### Andarin's Aegis

Andarin's Aegis is a large academy located in the temple district of a bustling metropolis. The Sheltering Hands established the academy as a school where its members can learn more about Abjuration magic, but, consistent with Sheltering Hands impulses, it has been used when needed as a soup kitchen, an impromptu medical center, a veteran's service center, and a shelter for children without homes. Its students are eager to learn from those who use Abjuration magic to aid others.

Andarin was one of the faction's earliest council members. Centuries after his death, many members still cite his dedication to helping the unhoused, but fewer remember his innovative contributions to the corpus of Abjuration spells.

The academy's dean is Malvanar Lumblin (Medium, Neutral Good **Aberrant Cultist**), a garrulous human who has reformed his wicked ways. Students hang on his words when he talks about the beholder cult he once served. Malvanar's expertise about the antimagic nature of beholder eyes has proven revolutionary to abjurative studies.
