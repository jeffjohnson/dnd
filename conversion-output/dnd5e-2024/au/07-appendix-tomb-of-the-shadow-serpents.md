# Appendix: Tomb of the Shadow Serpents


![Art: Jordan Kerbow](book/AU/079-07-001.appendix-splash.webp)

> An Adventure For **Level 1** Characters
> This adventure is designed to fill one or two sessions of play. It can take place in any region where there are forests and caves.

*[Page 151]*

## Key Plot Points

The following information is key to this adventure's story:

1. **Venger's Schemes** The characters receive a mysterious missive from **Venger** (see **chapter 6**), a fiendish mage who invites them to plumb the Tomb of the Shadow Serpents in search of treasure. In reality, **Venger** wishes to test whether the characters would be useful tools for his schemes.
2. **Escape the Tomb** The characters venture into the tomb and discover many fallen heroes who previously tried to claim **Venger** treasure. If the characters survive, they can keep the magic items they find, and they pique Venger's interest. If they die, their bones will lie in the tomb forever.

## Adventure Background

The fiendish, immortal mage **Venger** weaves interlocking schemes across the D&D multiverse to amass power and thwart his enemies. Some of Venger's schemes involve allying with—or opposing—heroes he manipulates to his own ends.

To identify heroes who might prove useful in future plots, **Venger** has built tombs across the multiverse as testing grounds. When heroes' deeds catch his attention, he invites those heroes to claim the treasure inside the tombs. **Venger** often challenges heroes to explore numerous tombs before he's convinced of their usefulness.

The offer of treasure is real—though **Venger** considers the treasures in his tombs inconsequential, adventurers might find them worth the risk. But the tombs are deadly, and they've become the final resting place for many adventurers who've failed Venger's challenges.

Among the earliest tombs **Venger** built is the Tomb of the Shadow Serpents. It's situated within a cave on the Material Plane near an intersection with the Shadowfell, so strange and gloomy environs envelop the tomb. The tomb holds deadly monsters and traps. Surviving the Tomb of the Shadow Serpents is the first step toward earning Venger's respect.

*[Page 152]*

### Adventure Hooks

For this adventure, your characters need to know only that a powerful individual has challenged them to explore the Tomb of the Shadow Serpents in search of treasure. You might make it clear that **Venger** has invited them, or you could obscure his identity, allowing the characters to deduce the tomb's creator while they explore or even once they've completed the adventure.

If you want to further entice the characters, use one of the following adventure hooks to draw them into the story:

1. **A Deadly Rivalry** The characters are embroiled in competition with another group and wish to prove their superior skills. Perhaps the characters are working for one of the magical factions described in **chapter 4** and wish to claim the tomb's treasure before another faction does. Or perhaps the characters have caught wind that rivals who compete with them for quests are also headed toward the tomb, and the characters hope to claim its treasure first. Regardless of the details, the characters can claim treasure—and bragging rights—if they survive the Tomb of the Shadow Serpents.
2. **Impress a Patron** The characters are cultivating a relationship with a powerful patron who might fund their continued adventures. Perhaps the characters want to impress a potential patron—such as one of the group patrons described in **chapter 3**—or they're trying to cement a fledgling relationship with a patron. Either way, the patron loves magical lore and tales of adventure. Emerging victorious from the Tomb of the Shadow Serpents is sure to get the patron's attention and prove the characters' capabilities.

## Beginning the Adventure

The characters receive a message from **Venger**. This might reach the characters in a nonmagical way, such as a letter delivered by a messenger, or it might reach them via magic such as a **Magic Mouth** or **Programmed Illusion** spell.

For players excited to play an adventure involving **Venger**, consider beginning with a *Programmed Illusion* spell (spell save DC 19) that activates when the characters reach the forest in which the tomb is located. When the illusion activates, the characters see a winged, one-horned fiend astride a flying black horse. The fiend swoops down from above and delivers the message in a deep, booming voice before flying away again.

If the journey to the tomb is more than a short distance away, include directions in this message as necessary:

> *[Page 153]*
>
> "Would-be heroes! Should you desire fame and treasure, heed this challenge. My Tomb of the Shadow Serpents lies nearby, built within a cave of unrelieved gloom. Seek out the stone doors that bear a serpent; within lies what you seek."
>
> "I eagerly await heroes worthy of claiming the tomb's riches. Many die terrible deaths within, but perhaps you shall be different. Test your mettle in my tomb, and perhaps one day we shall meet again to our mutual benefit."

If Venger's message is delivered in writing, it is signed with a capital *V*.

## Tomb of the Shadow Serpents

The Tomb of the Shadow Serpents has served **Venger** for generations as a testing ground for adventurers. Before inviting adventurers to brave it, **Venger** resets or repairs the tomb's features and replenishes its treasure to ensure there are always challenges and rewards for heroes who survive it. He never gives another thought to those who die here, leaving their bodies to litter the tomb.

*[Page 153]*

### Tomb Features

The Tomb of the Shadow Serpents consists of a mix of rooms with carved stone walls and natural cave chambers. Areas **T1**, **T5**, **T8**, **T9**, and **T10** are cave chambers, while areas **T2**, **T3**, **T4**, **T6**, **T7**, **T11**, and **T12** are constructed from stone.

The tomb has the following additional features.

*[Page 153]*

#### Ceilings

The ceilings of the tomb's cave chambers are 30 feet high. The ceilings of stone chambers are 20 feet high.

*[Page 153]*

#### Doors

Doors are made of stone and unlocked, except for the door that leads to the sepulcher (area **T11**), which is locked with the serpent puzzle described in "**Serpent Puzzle**."

*[Page 153]*

#### Fallen Adventurers

Throughout the tomb are the bodies of adventurers who have fallen to the place's perils. These adventurers might be of any species appropriate for your setting—such as a mix of dwarves, humans, and orcs. Area descriptions include visual clues about how the adventurers died. Any character who notices a fallen adventurer can take the **Study** action to examine the body and make a DC 15 Intelligence (**Investigation**) check; on a successful check, they determine the adventurer succumbed to a nearby trap—or to a nearby monster, as in area T8.

*[Page 154]*

#### Lighting

In the tomb's stone chambers and in its cave entrance, sconces shaped like shadowy serpents fill the area with **Dim Light**. The tomb's interior cave chambers are areas of **Darkness**; descriptions of these areas assume characters have a way of seeing in the dark.

*[Page 154]*

#### Serpent Puzzle

![Serpent Mosaic (Art: Coupleofkooks)](book/AU/082-07-002.serpent-mosaic.webp)

The mosaic on the double door in area **T11** that leads to the sepulcher is identical to the mosaic on the door in area **T1**. The mosaic is shown below. Each mosaic depicts a stylized, shadowy serpent. The mosaic on the sepulcher door, however, is missing three obsidian pieces that combine to form the shape of the serpent. These puzzle pieces are scattered throughout the tomb.

Serpent puzzle pieces are located in areas **T6**, **T8**, and **T10**, as shown on the map. Each puzzle piece is made from obsidian and worth 10 GP. A character who examines a piece realizes it's a broken part of an obsidian mosaic identical to the one in the door that leads into the tomb. The characters must place the puzzle pieces correctly back into the broken mosaic (or bypass the puzzle; see area **T11**) before they can enter the sepulcher.

*[Page 154]*

#### Walls

The walls of cave chambers are made of earth and thick tree roots. The walls of stone chambers are made from slabs of solid stone.

*[Page 154]*

### Tomb Locations

The following locations are keyed to map 1.

*[Page 154]*

#### T1: Cave Entrance

When the characters are ready to venture into the cave described in "Reaching the Tomb," read or paraphrase the following:

> *[Page 154]*
>
> Three hulking, wolflike beasts lumber around this wide cave entrance, their red, malevolent eyes peering at you. Gray light flickering from sconces reflects off an obsidian mosaic in a double door at the back of the cave entrance. The mosaic depicts an S-shaped serpent.

Three **Worg** lurk here and begin combat when the characters move 10 feet into the cave.

The worgs recently adopted the cave entrance as their home, assuming that creatures living inside the double door would periodically emerge and provide the worgs with scrumptious meals. The characters are the worgs' first potential victims, and the hungry creatures fight to the death. The worgs don't know about **Venger** or care about the purpose of the tomb.

*[Page 154]*

##### Serpent Mosaic

The obsidian serpent mosaic on the double door is one foot wide and one foot tall. It's identical to the mosaic the characters must reconstruct to open the serpent puzzle door in area **T11**.

*[Page 154]*

#### T2: Tomb Entryway

> *[Page 154]*
>
> A stone double door stands in the east wall of this chamber, and another stands in the west wall. Flanking the path that leads deeper into the temple are two identical statues. Each is seven feet tall and depicts a one-horned, winged man wearing robes and an ominous grin.

These statues depict **Venger**, as any character familiar with the mage easily recognizes. Any character unfamiliar with **Venger** can take the **Study** action to examine a statue and make a DC 10 Intelligence (**History** or **Religion**) check to try to identify the statue's likeness.

*[Page 154]*

##### Statue Traps

The statues are magical traps. The first time a creature moves within 5 feet of a statue, that statue intones in a deep, commanding voice, "Clasp my hand for a boon."

If a creature touches a statue's hand, a fiery jolt of energy discharges and the creature makes a DC 12 Dexterity saving throw, taking 5 (`1d10`) Fire damage on a failed save or half as much damage on a successful one. On a success or failure, the statue's magical voice chuckles and shouts, "Expect no favors!" Each statue activates only once.

*[Page 156]*

##### Detecting the Traps

A **Detect Magic** spell reveals an aura of Evocation magic around each statue. As a **Search** action, a creature within 5 feet of a statue can examine it and make a DC 10 Wisdom (**Perception**) check, noticing that the statue's hand emits warmth. The traps can't be disarmed, but they can be avoided by simply not touching the statues' hands.

#### T3: Deadly Corridor

> *[Page 156]*
>
> A desiccated corpse wearing rusty armor lies sprawled on this corridor's floor. The circlet on the corpse's head is strangely pristine and glints in the sconces' light, and a serpent-shaped blade protrudes from the body. Double doors lead east, west, and south.

This T-shaped corridor leads deeper into the tomb.

*[Page 156]*

##### Blade Traps

There are two pressure plates in this corridor, as denoted on the map. When a creature moves onto a pressure plate for the first time on a turn or starts its turn there, a gap opens in the adjacent wall, and a serpent-shaped blade flies toward the creature. The creature on the plate must succeed on a DC 15 Dexterity saving throw or take 3 (`1d6`) Slashing damage. Each trap activates once per turn. Once a trap has activated three times, the trap ceases to function.

*[Page 156]*

##### Detecting and Disarming the Traps

As a **Search** action, a creature can examine a section of floor that forms a pressure plate and make a DC 15 Wisdom (**Perception**) check, detecting the pressure plate on a successful check. Wedging an **Iron Spikes** or similar object under the pressure plate prevents the trap from triggering.

*[Page 156]*

##### Fallen Adventurer

Except for the circlet, the fallen adventurer's equipment is dented and unusable.

*[Page 156]*

##### Treasure

The circlet on the corpse's head is a **Circlet of Blasting**.

*[Page 156]*

#### T4: Mud Pit

> *[Page 156]*
>
> Spanning most of this chamber is a large pit of bubbling mud, from which rise shimmering waves of heat. A rickety bridge made of chains and decaying wooden planks crosses the pit.

The chain bridge is **Difficult Terrain**. When a creature steps on the bridge for the first time on a turn or starts its turn there, it must succeed on a DC 12 Dexterity saving throw or fall into the mud pit. A creature makes this save only once per turn.

*[Page 156]*

##### Mud Pit

The area of the mud pit acts like a 10-foot-deep quicksand pit hazard (see the *Dungeon Master's Guide*), but the mud is also dangerously hot thanks to a natural underground hot spring. Any creature that starts its turn in the mud takes 3 (`1d6`) Fire damage.

*[Page 156]*

##### Treasure

The western alcove is full of detritus covered in dried mud. A thorough search of the alcove takes 1 minute and yields a **Potion of Fire Breath**.

*[Page 156]*

#### T5: Spiderling Den

> *[Page 156]*
>
> The air in this dark, enormous cave is cool and damp. Thick spiderwebs cover the center of the floor and the entrance to a tunnel leading away to the east. A lumpy, five-foot-wide sac of webbing bulges from the eastern wall next to the tunnel's entrance.

Six juvenile giant spiders (use the **Giant Wolf Spider** stat block) lurk within the webs in the center of this chamber. They're the young of the giant spiders in area T10. A character who takes a **Search** action to survey the webs and succeeds on a DC 17 Wisdom (**Perception**) check notices the spiders.

As soon as a character ventures within 10 feet of the webs, the spiders emerge and begin combat. They fight to the death.

*[Page 156]*

##### Webs

Areas covered in webs, as denoted on the map, use the rules for the webs hazard (see **Webs**).

*[Page 156]*

##### Egg Sac

The sac on the eastern wall holds unhatched spider eggs. The sac has AC 10; HP 8; **Vulnerability** to Fire damage; and **Immunity** to Piercing, Poison, and Psychic damage. The sac is unaffected if the webs around it are destroyed.

Ten minutes after a character first enters this area, the eggs hatch and four **Spider** emerge. The spiders fight the characters on sight.

*[Page 156]*

#### T6: Long Corridor

> *[Page 156]*
>
> On this corridor's floor slump three corpses with darts protruding from their flesh. The center body clutches a shiny, black object in one hand.

Bloodstains splatter the floor of this hallway.

*[Page 156]*

##### Dart Traps

There are three pressure plates in this corridor, as denoted on **the map**. Each pressure plate triggers a poisoned darts trap (see the **Poisoned Darts**).

*[Page 157]*

##### Fallen Adventurers

The adventurers' equipment is dented and unusable.

*[Page 157]*

##### Serpent Puzzle Piece

The body in the center of the corridor clutches a piece of the serpent mosaic in area **T11**. When the characters find this piece, show them the *Serpent Puzzle Piece 1 handout*.

*[Page 157]*

#### T7: Hall of Armor

> *[Page 157]*
>
> Resting on a dais at the west end of this chamber are two suits of shining plate armor. Each suit is arranged on a stand whose wooden base is adorned with silver filigree. A few stairs lead up to the dais.

Both suits are **Animated Armor**. As soon as a character ventures within 5 feet of the stairs, the suits begin combat.

When a character is within 5 feet of an animated armor, they notice a seal etched on the armor's chest that depicts the ominously grinning face of a one-horned fiend—identical to the faces of the statues of **Venger** in area **T2**.

*[Page 157]*

##### Treasure

The wooden base of each armor stand includes a small drawer. The drawers are locked. As a **Utilize** action, a character can try to pick the lock using **Thieves' Tools**, doing so with a successful DC 15 Dexterity (**Sleight of Hand**) check.

The drawer in the south stand holds two obsidian stones worth 10 GP each. The drawer in the north stand holds a **Bag of Tricks** (tan).

*[Page 157]*

#### T8: Lacedons' Lair

> *[Page 157]*
>
> This wide cavern's walls are slick with trickling water that forms a murky, ten-foot-wide pool. To the east of the pool lies a one-armed corpse, next to which glints a shiny, black object. Strange gurgles emanate from the cavern's western end.

Two **Lacedon Ghoul** lurk in this cavern. One is around a corner in the western alcove, chewing on an arm it severed from the body in the northeast corner. Another is underwater in the pool, waiting to ambush trespassers.

As soon as a character ventures within 5 feet of the fallen adventurer or comes within 25 feet of the western wall, the underwater ghoul jumps out of the pool and fights them. The sounds of combat beginning alert the ghoul in the western alcove; that ghoul drops the arm it's chewing on and joins the fight.

*[Page 157]*

##### Fallen Adventurer

The ghouls killed an adventurer and have been snacking on the corpse, which is covered in bite marks. The adventurer's equipment is dented and unusable.

*[Page 158]*

##### Murky Pool

The pool is 5 feet deep and filled with cool, mineral-laden water.

*[Page 158]*

##### Serpent Puzzle Piece

Next to the body is a piece of the serpent mosaic in area T11. When the characters find this piece, show them the *Serpent Puzzle Piece 2 handout*.

> *[Page 158]*

> Thick spiderwebs cover the floor, walls, and ceiling of this tunnel.

The area of this tunnel is a webs hazard (see the *Dungeon Master's Guide*).

#### T10: Spiders' Nest

> *[Page 158]*
>
> Thick webs spill from this long cave's western opening. Perched on gossamer mounds of webbing to the north and south are two enormous spiders with hairy legs. On the northeast wall, a severed hand suspended in the webs holds a shiny, black object.

There are two **Giant Spider** here: one in the northeast corner and one in the southeast alcove. As soon as the characters enter this cave, the spiders fight them.

The spiders are hungry and fight to the death. They long ago ate the body the severed hand came from.

*[Page 158]*

##### Serpent Puzzle Piece

The severed hand holds a piece of the serpent mosaic in area T11. When the characters find this piece, show them the *Serpent Puzzle Piece 3 handout*.

#### T11: Serpent Puzzle Door

> *[Page 158]*
>
> The stone double door at the west end of this hall bears a mosaic similar to the one outside the tomb. But the space that held an obsidian, S-shaped serpent in the entrance's mosaic is empty in this one. Softly glowing blue lines illuminate the serpent-shaped void.

This door's magical lock is linked to the mosaic. The glowing lines in the mosaic's empty spaces thrum with magic.

*[Page 158]*

##### Serpent Mosaic

The mosaic is one foot wide and one foot tall. Placing the three missing pieces of the mosaic's obsidian serpent in the mosaic unlocks the door. The pieces are located in the long corridor (area **T6**), the lacedons' lair (area **T8**), and the spiders' nest (area **T10**).

Alternatively, a character can take a **Magic** action to trace the glowing lines and simulate the puzzle pieces' correct placement. A character who does so makes a DC 18 Intelligence (**Arcana**) check. On a successful check, the door unlocks.

#### T12: The Shadowy Sepulcher

![The shadows that haunt the sepulcher were once heroes who betrayed {@creature Venger|AU}. (Art: Jordan Kerbow)](book/AU/087-07-007.shadows.webp)

> *[Page 158]*
>
> Before you stands an eight-foot-tall central statue, around which swirl tendrils of wispy, black shadow. The statue is made of faceted obsidian and depicts entwined serpents whose heads rise toward the ceiling. On a dais to the west are three sarcophagi, their marble lids carved with the likenesses of warriors in repose.

Three **Shadow** lurk in this room, each hidden in one of the sarcophagi. As soon as a character ventures west of the serpent statue, the shadows emerge and begin combat.

The shadows arose from the bodies of adventurers **Venger** killed for betraying him. The mage then bound the shadows to this room. In a cruel act of irony, **Venger** built elaborate sarcophagi for the adventurers' bodies and placed them here, taunting the shadows with the prospect of eternal rest.

*[Page 158]*

##### Serpent Statue

**Venger** built the serpent statue and imbued it with magic he drew from the Shadowfell. As a result, the statue causes this room to behave as if the room were on that plane. It also causes the shadows to take on a serpentine appearance.

A character who enters this room must succeed on a DC 10 Wisdom saving throw or be affected by despair. Roll on the Shadowfell Despair table in the **Table: XDMG** to determine the form the despair takes.

The statue has AC 15, HP 18, and **Vulnerability** to Radiant damage. If the statue is reduced to 0 HP, the Shadowfell stops affecting this room, and any despair effects on the characters end immediately.

A character who takes the **Study** action to examine the statue and succeeds on a DC 15 Intelligence (**Arcana**) check understands how the statue is affecting the room and surmises that its connection to the Shadowfell gives it **Vulnerability** to Radiant damage.

*[Page 158]*

##### Treasure

The sarcophagus lids are made of solid marble. A character can take a **Utilize** action to make a DC 12 Strength (**Athletics**) check, lifting one of the lids on a successful check.

Inside each sarcophagus are the desiccated remains of an adventurer, whose equipment is rusted and unusable. The north sarcophagus also holds a bloodstone worth 50 GP. The central sarcophagus holds a **Cube of Summoning**. The south sarcophagus holds three turquoises worth 10 GP apiece.

## Conclusion

Once the characters are finished exploring the Tomb of the Shadow Serpents, award each character 100 XP for completing the adventure. The characters should have enough XP to advance to level 2 by the end of the adventure. If you're using a method of level advancement that doesn't rely on XP, the characters gain a level after defeating the shadows in the sepulcher.

If the characters survive the Tomb of the Shadow Serpents, **Venger** notices their prowess. He tracks the characters throughout their adventuring careers and might challenge them to explore other tombs he's created throughout the multiverse.

Dungeons in several published D&D adventures can be easily repurposed as tombs that **Venger** challenges the characters to explore. The temple in the level 4 adventure "The Will of Orcus" in *Dragon Delves*, for example, could be reimagined as such a tomb, with the cultists of the demon lord Orcus instead becoming sycophantic followers of **Venger**. The Tomb of the Nine Gods in *Tomb of Annihilation*, meant for level 9 adventurers, could similarly be reimagined as a complex created by **Venger** instead of the lich Acererak. The Tomb of Wayward Souls in *Vecna: Eve of Ruin*, another dungeon created by Acererak, is appropriate for level 16 characters. You can also create new tombs that **Venger** challenges the characters to explore.

At your discretion, after the characters have survived the dangers of numerous tombs, **Venger** might offer the characters mysterious jobs such as finding an item, stopping or delivering a shipment of goods, or protecting a public figure. These schemes should feed into Venger's attempts to thwart the League of Malevolence, an odious assemblage of power-hungry villains led by Venger's former lackey, the human mage Kelek. The characters shouldn't trust **Venger**, but they might accept his jobs (which always pay well) out of curiosity or as part of a plan to double-cross him.
